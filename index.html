Kenya refugee employment & documentation guide

Key frameworks under the Refugees Act, 2021 and the Department of Refugee Services (DRS).

1. Right to work (Refugees Act, 2021)

Recognized refugees in Kenya have the right to engage in gainful employment, start businesses, and access financial services, subject to the relevant permits and documents.

2. Essential identification

  • Refugee ID card: issued by DRS to recognized refugees aged 18+.
  • Proof of registration: for registered asylum seekers awaiting status determination.
  • KRA PIN: needed for formal payroll and tax.

3. Class M work permit

Employers hiring recognized refugees apply for a Class M permit through the Immigration eFNS portal, on DRS recommendation. Check the current fee and requirements with Immigration before applying.

4. Recognising foreign qualifications

  • KNQA (Kenya National Qualifications Authority) assesses and recognises foreign qualifications.
  • CUE (Commission for University Education) recognises foreign university degrees.
  • No papers? Start with alternative evidence on HireRefugee, then ask a partner for help with the formal route.

5. Getting paid

Salary can be paid to a bank account or M-Pesa. Requirements for accepting a refugee ID differ by provider, so confirm with the provider and bring your Refugee ID and KRA PIN.

Official sources

  • Department of Refugee Services (DRS)
  • UNHCR Kenya Help
  • Immigration Services
  • KNQA • CUE • KRA

General guidance only, not legal advice. Rules and fees change; always confirm with the official source.

Hiring a refugee in Kenya: your checklist

  1. Confirm the candidate's Refugee ID or proof of registration.
  2. Check their verified credentials and skills badges here.
  3. Request the Class M permit through Immigration (eFNS), on DRS recommendation.
  4. Ask the candidate to get a KRA PIN; register them for payroll, NSSF and SHIF.
  5. Pay by bank or M-Pesa; keep a signed contract and payslips.

General guidance. Confirm current rules with Immigration, DRS and KRA.
